## 1. Historical discovery

Constantin Thiopoulos, “Towards a Logic of Semiotic Systems,” *Mathématiques et sciences humaines* 117 (1992), 49–60, is a direct antecedent to several broad ideas in our programme.

The paper:
- argues that meaning should be treated as an entity's interlinking/positional role in a network, not merely as a denotational mapping;
- describes semiotic systems as weighted networks of terms;
- formalizes them using categories and weighted morphisms;
- defines an intensional interpretation of an object by the network of incoming weighted relations;
- defines context/situation categories as substructures generated by activated terms;
- defines hypercategories whose objects are semiotic categories and whose arrows are functors;
- uses structural invariants between situational categories to model consensual domains between coupled systems.

The conceptual overlap with relational semantics is substantial. It predates the current Semplex/Synesism programme by decades. Its philosophical commitments are explicitly phenomenological/autopoietic (Maturana, Varela, Heidegger, Merleau-Ponty), so it should not be treated as identical to our intended naturalized/physically grounded interpretation. The formal and conceptual overlap must nevertheless be acknowledged.

## 2. A specific formal obstruction in the 1992 threshold construction

Definitions 2.20–2.21 assign multiplicative morphism weights:
\[
w(f\circ g)=w(f)w(g)
\]
and propose the threshold family
\[
R_B(X)=\{f:Y\to X\mid w(f)\ge B\}
\]
as a sieve/topological interpretation (Theorem 2.1).

But a sieve must be closed under precomposition. The smallest counterexample uses:
\[
Q\xrightarrow{u}P\xrightarrow{g}X,\qquad h=g\circ u.
\]
Take:
\[
w(u)=0.5,\quad w(g)=0.9,\quad w(h)=0.45,
\]
with identity arrows assigned weight 1. At \(B=0.8\), the threshold family on \(X\) is \(\{id_X,g\}\), but precomposing \(g\) by \(u\) yields \(h\), whose weight is below threshold. Therefore the family is not a sieve. The exact sieve closure is \(\{id_X,g,h\}\).

The displayed proof of Theorem 2.1 appears to use the reverse implication: from \(w(f\circ g)\ge B\) it infers \(w(f)\ge B\) (which may hold when \(w(g)\le1\)). That is not the sieve axiom. The sieve axiom requires that \(w(f)\ge B\) imply \(w(f\circ g)\ge B\).

There is also a typing/definition issue in Definition 2.17 as printed: a Grothendieck topology assigns a collection of covering sieves to each object, whereas the formula in Definition 2.21 appears to assign a single threshold sieve per object. A charitable reconstruction may have been intended, but the printed statement is not precise enough to remove the sieve-closure problem.

This critique is narrow. It does not invalidate the article's broader semiotic intuition, its weighted-network proposal, or every possible repair of its formalism.

## 3. General closure criterion

For fixed \(B\), the threshold family \(R_B(X)\) is a sieve iff:
\[
\forall f:Y\to X,\forall u:Z\to Y,\quad
w(f)\ge B\Rightarrow w(f\circ u)\ge B.
\]

Under multiplicativity, this is:
\[
w(f)\ge B\Rightarrow w(f)w(u)\ge B.
\]

If the condition is required for every \(B>0\), choose \(B=w(f)>0\); then \(w(f)w(u)\ge w(f)\), so \(w(u)\ge1\). Conversely, if every composable pre-arrow has weight at least 1, multiplication preserves threshold membership. Thus nontrivial normalized weights in \((0,1]\) generally violate the condition.

The finite script checks the concrete case and records the full threshold frontier:
- \(B=0.4\) and \(B=0.45\): the maximal sieve appears in this three-arrow example;
- \(B=0.5,0.8,0.9\): the threshold family excludes \(h\) and fails sieve closure.

## 4. The resource-cost analogue

With costs \(c(g)=1\), \(c(h)=3\), the budget sublevel at \(B=1\) includes \(g\) but not its precomposition \(h\). Its sieve closure adds \(h\) and has closure cost 3.

This demonstrates a structural conflict:
- a resource threshold can select a useful experiment;
- the corresponding family may not be closed under categorical refinement;
- taking its sieve closure can add operations that exceed the physical budget;
- therefore the abstract restricted-Yoneda/sieve construction cannot simply assume all categorical refinements are physically available.

This is the precise point where a resource-indexed operational semantics would need additional structure.

## 6. Consequences for Semplex/Synesism

1. The 1992 paper must be included as a major conceptual antecedent for structural/relational semantics and weighted categorical semiotics.
2. We cannot claim to have invented “meaning as network position,” weighted semiotic categories, contextual situation categories, or category-theoretic modelling of semiotic systems.
3. The paper's multiplicative threshold construction does not establish a general sieve unless additional monotonicity assumptions are supplied.
4. The resource-bounded executable-probe problem remains meaningful, but it is now explicitly a problem of reconciling operational feasibility, precomposition closure, and budget.
5. The correct next step is to compare alternative constructions: executable sieves with admissible-refinement subcategories; costed coverages with pullback/refinement cost laws; and enriched/quantale-valued categories. None should be called novel before a dedicated prior-art search.
